Details and checks

Methods and definitions: rollout sampling (temperature 1, no top-k or top-p), χ, r, classes (over, under, middle), Δ slope, name quiz

Rollouts. Each model writes unprompted from <|endoftext|> at temperature 1, with no top-k or top-p, although post-trained configs recommend 0.6 and 0.95. A post-trained rollout ends at the model's first <|im_end|> or <|endoftext|>. Special-token ids are removed before encoding, since Dolma's SAE inputs contain none.

χ and r. χ is a concept's coverage relative to the average concept (CG paper Eq. 2). r = ln χset − ln χtrain. All sets in a comparison use the same number of documents, since fewer documents alone steepen the curve. Slope statistics are over the slope concepts (G13).

Classes and lists. Per model, concepts with χtrain ≥ e−5 are ranked by r: lowest 10% = under, highest 10% = over, the rest = middle (CG paper Sec. 3.1, 5.1). Random lists: 80 per class. Extreme lists: the 50 highest and 50 lowest r, unfiltered and among concepts with at least 50 firing docs on both sides.

Name quiz. 200 list entries, 100 per group (base = stage1, main; instruct = SFT, DPO, Instruct). Each gets a name from Dolma windows and one from that model's rollout windows, and both are quizzed on the same held-out Dolma V windows (30 firing + 30 non-firing, balanced accuracy, Paulo et al. detection). Design: exp2_rollout_gap/design.md G70-G74. Window text stays off this page (G20).

Shuffle vs shape table: rank-shuffle share, shape share, Spearman ρ, Var(r), trend share, excess dispersion sd

Dpair = mean r² (concepts paired by id). Dmarg = the same after sorting each side, so it sees only the two histograms. Shape share = Dmarg / Dpair; rank-shuffle share = the rest. Var(r) splits into a trend part, (b − 1)² Var(ln χtrain) from the line fit of ln χset on ln χtrain, and the scatter around that line; the trend share is the part a slope can describe. Excess dispersion sd = √(Var(r) − the set's noise − train's noise), noise from split halves, in ln units; V defines train's noise (G40, G42).

Δ slope robustness: all docs, length-matched, garbled removed, count control

Bold: bias-corrected Δ (G45). Below it: raw Δ and its 95% basic bootstrap interval (G17). Each column applies one document choice to every set, V included. Length-matched: in each length bin, every set cut to the smallest count (G43). Garbled removed: docs whose mean token NLL under the encoder model is above V's 99th percentile dropped (G58). Count control: random drops to the garbled-removed count, 20 draws (G60). Count control minus all docs is what the smaller count does alone; garbled removed minus count control is what the flag does.

Details and checks, missing concepts

Methods and definitions, missing concepts: e⁻⁵ cutoff, list rules, random 80, 50 most extreme, 50-doc filter, naming, theme coding

Lists. Per model, all 65,536 concepts qualify, with no firing floor on the missing side. In Dolma, missing from rollouts: χtrain ≥ e−5 and χrollout < e−5. Concepts move with their nearest neighbours

Rank correlation between a concept's r and its neighbours' mean r. Held-out Dolma shows sampling noise alone; random concepts are matched in frequency.

  • Each dot asks: when a concept's frequency changes from Dolma to a model's rollouts (r), do its 11 most similar concepts change the same way? The score is a rank correlation (Spearman ρ): 1 means they always move together, 0 means unrelated.
  • All five Olmo 3 models score ρ = 0.72-0.78 (mid-trained lowest at 0.72, SFT and Instruct highest at 0.78). Random concepts with the same training frequency score 0.01-0.04, so the link comes from similarity, not frequency.
  • Held-out Dolma, which involves no model and shows only sampling noise, scores 0.39. Similar concepts share some noise, and the models' co-movement is about twice that.

Similar concepts rarely take each other's place

Share of under-represented concepts whose nearest neighbour is over-represented, against random concepts of matched training frequency.

  • If a model replaced a concept with a near-duplicate, the concept would be under-represented and its most similar concept over-represented. Each dot is the share of under-represented concepts where that happens.
  • It happens for 3.5% of under concepts in the mid-trained model, 1.7% in the pretrained model and 1.1-1.3% in the post-trained models, against 11-14% when the partner is a random concept of the same training frequency.
  • Similar concepts mostly rise or fall together (chart above), so one taking another's place is rare.

No clear clusters beyond one 2-way split

  • Each concept is linked to its 11 most similar concepts, and the curve shows this graph's eigenvalues. A large jump after the K-th eigenvalue would mean the concepts fall into K well-separated groups.
  • Among 2 or more groups, the largest jump is after the 2nd eigenvalue (0.078 to 0.111), only slightly larger than the next (0.111 to 0.137); past that, eigenvalues climb in small steps to 0.357 at the 101st. Splitting in two gives groups of 45,406 and 20,130 concepts.
  • Gap concepts are spread evenly over the two groups: about 10% of an under concept's group-mates are also under, the same as with shuffled labels (10.1%). Over concepts reach 10.4-10.8% in the post-trained models against 10.2% shuffled, less than one percentage point higher.

Gap concepts gather in parts of the concept tree, and so does noise

The concept graph is split in two again and again, down to depth 8 (183 groups at that depth). At each level, a group is "above the null" when its share of under- (or over-) represented concepts beats 97.5% of 100 shuffles that keep each concept's training frequency. Depths 1-4 have only 2-16 groups, so their percentages jump in large steps.

  • If gap concepts were scattered at random, about 2.5% of groups would be above the null. At depth 8, 23-25% of groups are above it for under-represented concepts and 22-28% for over-represented concepts, in every model.
  • Held-out Dolma, which involves no model, already reaches 18% (under) and 17% (over), so most of this clustering comes from similar concepts sharing sampling noise (as in the co-movement chart). The models add 4-11 percentage points on top.
  • Over-represented groups are more model-specific: at depth 8, 4-9 of the 36-46 over groups per model are also above the null in held-out Dolma, against 12-16 of the 37-41 under groups.

A second similarity measure: text embeddings of where each concept fires

Each concept's 40 Dolma training docs where it fires most give 40 short windows (32 tokens around the peak). Qwen3-Embedding-8B turns each window into a vector, the 40 vectors are averaged, and two concepts are similar when these averages point the same way. Decoder cosine (above) compares what the SAE writes into the model; this compares the kinds of text a concept fires in. Each concept again keeps its 11 nearest concepts.

Embedding neighbours move together as much as decoder neighbours, but share more noise

  • Same test as the first chart on this tab, with each concept's 11 nearest embedding neighbours in place of its decoder neighbours. All five models score ρ = 0.72-0.78 with either measure; random concepts of the same training frequency score 0.01-0.04.
  • Held-out Dolma, which shows only sampling noise, rises from 0.39 (decoder) to 0.56 (embedding). Embedding neighbours often peak in the same documents: 76% of neighbour pairs share at least one of their 40 top docs, against 31% of decoder pairs and 6% of random pairs, and concepts counted in the same docs share sampling noise.
  • So the models' co-movement beyond noise is smaller with embeddings (0.16-0.22 above held-out Dolma) than with decoder cosine (0.33-0.39). The two measures pick different neighbours: under 5% of embedding neighbours are also decoder neighbours.

Split in two, the embedding graph separates code and lists from prose, and the models lean to code and lists

  • Split in two by spectral clustering (as the decoder graph above), the embedding graph gives 20,117 concepts whose named members are mostly code, list separators and document structure, and 45,419 mostly in ordinary prose. These labels come from names, not checked against text.
  • In every model, 28-33% of the code-and-lists half is over-represented (the dashed line is the 10% expected overall) and 2-4% of the prose half; under-represented concepts go the other way, 12-13% of the prose half and 3-5% of the code-and-lists half.
  • Held-out Dolma has more of both in the code-and-lists half (20% under, 17% over), so that half is noisier, but noise raises both sides while the models raise only over. The decoder graph's two halves showed no such lean.

Two groups: list-like and repetitive text rises only after stage 1; scientific prose is low from stage 1 on

  • Groups picked by a fixed rule: among the pretrained model's 10 tree groups with the largest excess (dropdown below), the one with the highest share; that model's bar is therefore the selected one. Group 13's named members are separators between list items, tokens that repeat a pattern just set up and positions before a number. Group 279's are citation markers, pieces of biochemical names and methods text in scientific papers.
  • Group 13: 55-71% over-represented in the pretrained and post-trained models, but 16% at the end of stage 1 and 13% in held-out Dolma. The rise falls between the end of stage 1 and the final base model, the step that adds the stage 2 and 3 data (dolmino, longmino), which the Dolma reference leaves out.
  • Group 279: 37% under-represented already at the end of stage 1, 36% in the pretrained model and 25-30% after post-training, against 17% in held-out Dolma. Rollouts rarely read like scientific papers, a large part of Dolma (olmOCR science PDFs are 13.6% of stage 1 tokens).

By their names, embedding neighbours are related more often for topic concepts

  • The blind check from the decoder dropdown, repeated: a rater saw only the one-sentence names of 100 nearest-neighbour pairs mixed with 100 random pairs. Each concept was also typed as topic (tied to one kind of document, such as code or chemistry) or token (punctuation, positions or formatting found in any text).
  • Overall 57 of 100 embedding pairs were rated related, against 50 of 100 decoder pairs; random pairs got 1 and 2. When both concepts are topic concepts, 31 of 36 embedding pairs are related (86%) against 26 of 43 decoder pairs (60%).
  • For two token concepts the measures are close (18 of 36 and 18 of 33), so the expected weakness of embeddings on token concepts did not show. One rater (Claude), names not checked against text, 24-43 pairs per type.

Details and checks, concept similarity

Methods and definitions, concept similarity: decoder cosine, 11 neighbours, co-movement test, swap test, spectral clustering, eigengap

Similarity. Cosine of the SAE's decoder rows (W_dec), as Exp 1 found nearest neighbours. Each concept keeps its 11 nearest other concepts (11 ≈ ln 65,536).

Co-movement. u = rank of r among concepts with χtrain ≥ e−5, scaled to 0-1; Spearman ρ between u and the mean u of the concept's ranked neighbours, 1,000 bootstrap resamples. Baselines: held-out Dolma in place of the model, and 11 random concepts within ±500 χtrain ranks in place of the neighbours.

Swap. Among under concepts, the share whose nearest ranked neighbour is over (and the reverse), same baselines. A high share would mean a near-duplicate direction took the concept's place ("feature absorption", Chanin et al. 2024).

Clusters. Graph linking each concept to its 11 neighbours, weighted by cosine; normalized spectral clustering (Ng, Jordan and Weiss 2002), as Li et al. (2024) cluster SAE features; number of clusters by the largest eigengap among the first 100 eigenvalues (von Luxburg 2007). Design: exp2_rollout_gap/design.md G101-G109.

Methods, concept tree: repeated 2-way splitting, stopping rule, frequency-matched shuffle test

Tree. Starting from all 65,536 concepts, each group of the 11-neighbour graph is split in two by spectral clustering (Shi and Malik 2000), down to depth 8; a group whose links fall into separate pieces splits into those pieces instead, and groups under 22 concepts are not split.

Test. For each group with at least 10 ranked concepts, the share that are under (or over) is compared with 100 shuffles of the labels within bins of 1,000 concepts of similar training frequency; a group is above the null when its share beats the 97.5th percentile. Held-out Dolma in place of a model shows the noise level. Design: exp2_rollout_gap/design.md G110-G113.
